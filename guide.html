JAWS Project

McKibben muscle jaw, Soft Robotics Clinic, Rowan University. Built Fall 2026 for SECII, Spring 2027.

This page has the links and files for our jaw project. Students use these all semester, and the plan below shows which page goes with which week. Everything runs in a web browser, so there is nothing to install and no sign-in. All pressures are in psi.

Pages

LinkWhat it does
ScoreboardLive results for the projector. Ranks teams by strongest bite, by muscle orientation, and by calculated vs. measured force. Students can open it on their phones too.
Team stationOne per team, on a laptop in Chrome or Edge. Connects to the load cell over USB and saves the peak force of every squeeze.
Jaw force calculatorUses the team's muscle model from the characterization page and the jaw measurements to predict the bite force for each orientation, with the uncertainty, the best muscle angle, and every step of the math shown. It also works Challenge 1 backward to a pressure, and can load a team's squeezes from the scoreboard to compare.
Muscle characterizationPaste in the force/pressure logger output. It fits F = P(c₁ − c₂ε), predicts muscle force for any pressure and contraction, and saves the model so the jaw calculator can use it.
Muscle pressure: leak testMeasures leakage: graphs pressure over time from the pressure logger after the muscle is filled, and gives the peak pressure and how fast it leaks (psi per second). It can also show how long the leak lets the jaw hold a bite.
Lift testSyringe volume and pressure in, mass and lift height out. Gives the energy put in, the work done, the efficiency, and the energy lost to hysteresis.

The two challenges

  • Challenge 1 (Weeks 7–9): each team gets a bite force and an opening angle. Use your muscle model and jaw model to find the pressure you need, with a range, and turn it in before you test. Section 4 of the jaw force calculator does the math and shows every step.
  • Challenge 2 (Week 10): the jaw has three variables: pressure, opening angle, and orientation (A–G). Your team is told which one to hold constant. Change the other two to get the most force, and use your model to decide what to try.

Setting up a team station

Do this once per rig before class. Each rig is an Arduino with an HX711 board and the jaw's load cell. The setup guide has the wiring and the database steps.

  1. Upload jaw_force_sensor.ino to the Arduino (once per board).
  2. Plug the Arduino into the team's laptop with USB. Close the Arduino IDE so it isn't holding the port.
  3. Open the team station in Chrome or Edge and pick the team's slot.
  4. With nothing touching the jaw, click Connect sensor and pick the Arduino's port. The board restarts and zeroes itself.
  5. First time only: open Calibrate this rig, press Zero, rest a known mass (for example 500 g) on the load cell, type the mass, and press Calibrate. The Arduino remembers it.
  6. Pick the orientation (A to G) being tested and type the team's calculated force for it.
  7. Squeeze. Every squeeze over 2 N saves its peak to the scoreboard automatically. Use Pause saving while setting up and Delete for a bad reading.

Week 4: force vs. pressure test

  1. Hang the muscle from the load cell, with the syringe, tee and pressure sensor on the same air line.
  2. Upload muscle_force_pressure.ino and open the Serial Monitor (115200 baud, Newline).
  3. With the muscle deflated, type t to zero both sensors.
  4. Set the muscle length with the turnbuckle, measure how much it has shortened, and type c and the number in mm (for example c12).
  5. Push the syringe up through about six pressures and type h at each one. Then come back down through a few.
  6. Do the same at two or more other lengths. Type n before repeating a trial.
  7. Paste everything from the Serial Monitor into the muscle characterization page and enter the muscle's length, diameter and braid turns.

Week 3: pressure and leak test

  1. Upload muscle_pressure_logger.ino to the UNO R4. The Serial Plotter shows the pressure live.
  2. In the Serial Monitor, type r, then push and hold the syringe. It records for 20 seconds.
  3. Copy the line that starts with DATA and paste it into the muscle pressure page. Paste more DATA lines to average several tests.
  4. Keep it under 9 psi so the sensor doesn't get damaged.

Weeks 4 and 10: lift test

  1. Hang a known mass from the muscle (Week 4) or from the jaw's bite point (Week 10). Tape a ruler behind it.
  2. Put the syringe on the air line instead of the compressor, so you can read the volume.
  3. Push the syringe one mark at a time and write down the volume and the pressure at each mark. Then come back down the same way.
  4. Measure how far the mass rose at the top.
  5. Type the readings, the mass, and the height into the lift test page.
